# ARAM

> *For the Legends of Runeterra game mode, see LoR:ARAM.*

ARAM Queue

**ARAM** (**All Random, All Mid**) is a permanent 5v5 Normal game queue and Classic game mode in League of Legends. It is played on Howling Abyss, Butcher's Bridge or Koeshin's Crossing, randomized every lobby with equal probability of appearing. During champion select, all ten players are given 2 or 3 random champions to pick from. Then, when in game, the two teams battle in one lane which resembles the middle lane of Summoner's Rift. Each side has four turrets, one inhibitor, their Nexus, as well as two health relics and a shop stall available. The objective is to destroy the enemy Nexus.

As of patch V14.22, players returning to *League of Legends* by playing ARAM games are matched against bots to ease their transition back into playing the game. Active players queuing with returning players will usually be matched with bots, and there is also always a chance they are matched with returning players thus facing bots as well.

With the launch of Trials of Twilight: Act II in V25.21, an altered version of ARAM called ARAM: Mayhem was released as a separate queue. In this mode, players are offered a unique spin on the ARAM experience that revolves around powering their champions up with *augments*, which are special effects they can equip to greatly enhance their champions' capabilities.

## History

### Origin

ARAM was initially a popular community-made spin on Custom games using Summoner's Rift, where it was often called *ARAM*, or Crystal Scar, where it was often called *ARAB*. In these matches, players agreed to select a random champion using the “Random Champion” button, buy items as usual and focus on 5v5 fights in a single area: usually the middle lane on Summoner's Rift (as it was the shortest path from spawn) or the bottom half of the Crystal Scar on Dominion (by consensus). The growing popularity of the mode led to a dedicated subset of the player base developing a tight-knit culture around the “ARAM/ARAB” rule set.

After the first-ever Thunderdome event at Riot in 2012, the Proving Grounds map—previously used solely for the original League of Legends tutorials—was revamped and re-released on June 29th, 2012 as a Custom game map, in addition to its use in tutorials. From that point onward, ARAM would feature an enforced and standardized ruleset, better suited for single-lane gameplay and shorter matches compared to Classic Summoner's Rift. Players began playing ARAM on this map instead of Summoner's Rift.

Following the conclusion of the Freljord event in April 2013 with the release of patch V3.6, the *Howling Abyss* was introduced, completely replacing the Proving Grounds in both tutorials and Custom games (the old tutorial remained in place until the launch of the modern tutorial). An official ARAM queue was also launched on the Howling Abyss map at the same time, and it has remained available ever since.

An installation of *Dev Doodles* about early development of ARAM was published on October 13th, 2025 on the official Youtube channel, viewable here.

### Butcher's Bridge & Experimentation

From July 22nd, 2015, until August 10th, 2015 (V5.14–V5.15), the Howling Abyss was reskinned for the Bilgewater-themed *Burning Tides: Part Two* event, creating the Butcher's Bridge. The (also) Bilgewater-themed *Curse of the Drowned* event, which ran from May 31st to June 18th, 2018 (V8.11–V8.12), brought back an updated version of Butcher's Bridge with visual improvements, four exclusive items ( Ghostwalkers, Bloodletter's Veil, Lifeline, and Spectral Cutlass), the addition of Health Relic heal beams, and the removal of the notoriously frustrating items Banner of Command and Warmog's Armor. After the event ended, a nerfed version of Warmog's Armor was reinstated but the exclusive items were removed. Some of them would later return in ARAM and other game modes.

Butcher's Bridge returned once again with patch V9.7, from April 4th to April 15th, 2019, for a dedicated "ARAM event". This gave Riot the opportunity to test more significant changes and additions to the mode, such as blind bans during champion select, adjusted minion rules, champion-specific balancing, a global anti–long-range poke mechanic, and more. The bans, exclusive items, and the summoner spell Backtrack were later reverted, but many other changes from this period have remained as permanent fundamentals of ARAM balancing and gameplay.

### Further Development

ARAM has been played on the Howling Abyss for the longest time, so much so that the map has become synonymous with the mode and its gameplay loop, from champion select to the end of the game. Since the queue's launch in 2013 and up until June 2025 (V25.13), the only change to champion select rules occurred in V8.11, when a "bench" was added that allows players to swap their rolled champion for any other one re-rolled by them or their team.

A number of significant changes and quality-of-life improvements were implemented alongside patch V12.23. The Howling Abyss lane was widened slightly, and *Frost Gates* were introduced, the Howling Abyss–themed equivalent of Hex-gates found on the Summoner's Rift Hextech Rift, to reduce repetitive travel time from the fountain to the action. Turrets received a revamp where they would collapse when destroyed, creating new terrain on the map called rubble, which partially constricted the lane. Players quickly voiced concerns and frustration about turret rubble, as the single narrow lane already limited fights, movement, and vision—giving zoning champions a constant advantage. In a blog post on February 16th, 2023, Riot acknowledged that turret rubble negatively impacted the player experience and stated that a change or removal is necessary. Turret rubble was ultimately removed in patch V13.5.

In addition to the changes introduced in V12.23, Riot hosted the first-ever ARAM Cup for Clash on December 10th and 11th, 2022 as a token of appreciation for the ARAM community. ARAM Cup tournaments were later incorporated into the regular Clash schedule, starting on August 19th and 20th, 2023 (V13.16).

Prior to 2024, the map had only changed on three occasions, each time being replaced by Butcher's Bridge. To celebrate the Arcane Season 2 event (V14.22–V14.24), Riot introduced the Bridge of Progress, a new spin on ARAM that featured unique buffs, custom terrain, and thematic visuals.

On June 9th, 2025, during Spirit Blossom Beyond, Riot published an article unveiling an experimental "map rotation" system, where players receive not only random champions but a random map too. Each map in the rotation features small to moderate changes or additions to the core ARAM gameplay on the Howling Abyss, alongside new visuals. For this purpose, a new map called *Koeshin's Crossing* was created, themed around  Spirit Blossom. Additionally, Butcher's Bridge returned once again, now featuring one Cannon Launcher next to each team's fountain and a restorative Power Flower, first introduced in Arena. Finally, the All Random draft in champion select was overhauled: instead of being forced to re-roll for potential choices one by one, all players are now presented all their possible champion options at once, from which they can choose one.

With the conclusion of Spirit Blossom Beyond in V25.17, the randomized map rotation—originally scheduled to end after 25.16—was continued, now with equalized chances for all maps. In a blog post released on October 6th, 2025, this map rotation system was made permanent following a ton of positive feedback.

## Overview

- **Draft type:** All random, blind, no bans

- **Maps:** Howling Abyss, Butcher's Bridge, Koeshin's Crossing

- **Mode:** Classic

- **Average Game Time:** 20 minutes

## Champion Select

Champion cards interface
"Blessing" info

In the first 15 seconds of champion select, a reveal phase takes place during which players will be presented a selection of two champions picked randomly from their available champion pool, with ***approximately*** a 5% – 30% (based on number of player's unlocked champions) chance of being *blessed* with three instead. The champions are presented as "cards" that can be clicked on by the player to acquire them. The third champion card to be offered is referred to as a "blessing" and is marked by a blue icon. After each player makes a selection from their offered cards, the unselected champions in their offerings will be moved to the allied team's Available Champion Pool (aka the *Bench*), shown at the top of the client.

Once 3 seconds have elapsed since making their initial selection, players can freely swap to a champion that was originally in their selection by clicking on them in the Bench, moving their current champion to the pool and becoming unable to swap again for 3 seconds if done so. Other allied players' unselected/swapped out champions will be visible in the Bench but be locked out of swapping to teammates for the remainder of the reveal phase. The reveal phase will end prematurely after 3 seconds of the last player making their selection. If a player does not make a selection by the end of the phase, one of the champions in their offering is selected randomly by the client.

After the reveal phase, there will be a 45-second finalization phase for players to prepare their loadout or choose a different champion from the Bench. All champions in the Bench will become unlocked to all players after 3 seconds of this phase ensuing. Players can swap to another champion in the Bench only if it is part of their available champion pool. When a player swaps out their current champion, it is moved to the Bench and a 3-second swap cooldown for that champion is incurred to other players, and the player cannot swap champions again for 3 seconds.

Available Champion Pool (Bench) interface

Teams are blind to the enemy team's picks and Bench. The same champion cannot appear twice between the two teams.

Available champions to be offered include:

- Unlocked champions.

- The current plus last 4 Free champion rotations combined (*free ARAM roster*).

  - This means a maximum of 100 champions, assuming no repeat appearances.

### Skin Boosts

In a matchmade game, players can grant a **Skin Boost** to their team, which unlocks a random skin for any champion they pick for one game only. Skin Boosts cost 95 and become available for purchase during the finalization phase of champion select. Only one Skin Boost can be active at a time.

- If the game is dodged, the used RP is refunded.

- Skins are pre-determined each lobby:

  - If the player swaps their pick to a different champion, that champion will also have a random skin unlocked.

  - Skins are not rolled again when the player picks the same champion a second time after a Skin Boost was purchased.

- The player who purchased a Skin Boost is announced in chat.

- Skin Boosts become unavailable for purchase in the last 10 seconds of the finalization phase.

Upon completing the game, the player who granted the team a Skin Boost is awarded 200, while their teammates receive 100 each.

### ARAM Warddrobe

Currently testing on the Oceania and Brazilian servers, players can pay 1350 to unlock all Epic-tier and below skins for every champion for one month in ARAM and ARAM: Mayhem. Once the subscription ends, they can renew it at the next period.

## Gameplay

All ARAM rules apply to both matchmade and Custom games.

### General Rules

- Players begin the game with 1400 gold.

- Gold generation starts at **1:00** and is equal to 60 per 10 seconds.

- Recall becomes non-functional.

- **Platform Regen Disabled:** The fountain does not restore any health or resources.

  - Health relics exist throughout the map instead.

- **Limited Shopping:** Item purchases and sales cannot be performed after exiting the shop area or entering combat. Shopping is enabled again only upon dying.

  - The distance at which the shop becomes unavailable is slightly beyond the Nexus turrets.

- Surrendering is available from **08:00** and can only pass with a majority Yes vote (4/5 or 70%).

Each ARAM map features a unique method of fast travel that can be interacted with. In the Howling Abyss, there are *Frost-gates* right in front of spawn, for example.

### Minions

- Minions start spawning at **0:50**.

- Area damage effects deal 75% damage to minions.

- Minion health upgrades changed to every 50 seconds.

- Waves spawn every 25 – 13 (based on minutes) seconds (speeding up in the late-game).

- Minions have 325 – 425 (based on minutes) movement speed (speeding up in the late-game).

- Siege minions spawn every two waves, starting with the third wave.

- Siege minions and super minions are passively empowered by *Blackout*.

  - **Passive Effect - Blackout:** When a stealthed trap is detected within 1100 units, reveal and expose all stealthed traps within the same area. *Blackout* lingers for 4 seconds after there are no longer any traps nearby.

- Minion-specific adjustments:

  - Melee minion

    - Base health changed to 455.

    - Base attack damage changed to 12.

    - Base movement speed changed to 325.

    - Base experience changed to 64.

  - Caster minion

    - Base health changed to 290.

    - Base attack damage changed to 23.

    - Base movement speed changed to 325.

    - Base experience changed to 32.

    - Base bounty changed to 17.

  - Siege minion

    - Base health changed to 805.

    - Base attack damage changed to 40.

    - Base movement speed changed to 325.

    - Base experience changed to 94.

    - Base bounty changed to 52.

  - Super minion

    - Base attack damage changed to 306.

    - Base attack speed changed to 0.694.

    - Attack speed ratio changed to 0.694.

    - Base armor changed to 60.

    - Base magic resistance changed to 10.

    - Base experience changed to 100.

    - Base bounty changed to 45.

    - Base movement speed changed to 325.

### Champions

- Champions begin the game at level 3.

- Champions receive 85% – 70% (based on distance) damage from enemy champions further than 1000 units away, excluding damage dealt by damage over time and ultimate abilities.

- Champions deal 0% – 25% (based on minutes) **bonus** damage to structures in the late-game.

- Champions gain a global buff ^e in all ARAM maps that does the following:

  - +70 summoner spell haste.

  - Restores  0.1% of **maximum** mana per second.

  - Passively gains  5 experience per second.

  - Reduces all outgoing heals by 50%. *The healing reduction does not affect self-heals.*

  - Omnivamp and  life steal healing effectiveness against minions is reduced by 40%.

  - While alive and within 1250 units of an enemy minion that dies, gain 6 from its death (unless you scored the killing blow on it).

  - Melee champions gain  15 **bonus** magic resistance and their basic attacks deal 20% increased damage against siege minions and super minions.

  - Melee champions deal 20% increased damage against structures, if they adaptively have more ability power. *This buff is replaced by the generic time-based ARAM buff's bonus damage to structures once the latter exceeds 20%.*

### Champion Gold Bounties

- Champion kills are worth 180 **base** gold.

- Bounties accrue at a rate of  +  1 bounty gold :  2 gold earned from kills and assists, up to 600 more than the base amount.

- Bounties depreciate at a rate of  −  1 bounty gold :  4 gold distributed to the killers and assistors.

- Assists always generate an additional 90, split among all assisting allies.

- First Blood generates 50% more gold toward the kill and assist bounties.

- All bounties are reduced to 50% – 100% (based on Game Time) early in the game.

### Death Timers

Champions respawn 11 / 13 / 15 / 17 / 19 / 21 / 22 / 24 / 26 / 28 / 30 / 32 / 34 / 36 / 38 / 40 seconds after dying.

## Reroll (Historical)

Before V25.13, each player could roll for a new champion that has not been rolled by anyone in the lobby via a Reroll button in the middle of the screen. This required a **reroll credit**.

When a player rerolled their champion, their previous one would be placed into their team's Available Champion Pool (AKA the *Bench*). The team's rerolled champions were added to the Bench sequentially. Any player could swap their champion with one from the *Bench* if they owned it or it was part of the Free champion rotation.

Each account could have up to 2 reroll credits, and a credit is rewarded for earning 250 *ARAM points*, earned by completing matchmade ARAM games. The number of points granted is based on the player's available champions; the ARAM free roster exists as a constant factor.

> `Reroll Points = 65 + 1.5 × 𝐴𝑣𝑎𝑖𝑙𝑎𝑏𝑙𝑒`

- 65 = ARAM free roster

- 1.5 = Multiplier

- *The result is rounded down to the nearest integer.*

**Notes**

- The number of points granted after every match can be seen on the post-game screen above the "Dice" icon tooltip.

- Any points that reach in excess of the credit reward goal will rollover to count towards a second credit, if available. A roll credit is ensured every two matches.

- To ensure a reroll credit every single match, a player needs to have unlocked at least 57 champions who are not part of the free ARAM roster. To ensure this at all times, a player needs 122 unlocked champions.

- Gaining **Leaver / AFK** status will disable point rewards for that match.

- If the game is dodged, any used reroll credits will be refunded to all other players.

  - This does not occur for the dodging player and their premade party, who will lose all spent reroll credits.
