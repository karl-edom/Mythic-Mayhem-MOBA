The **Rift Herald** is an epic monster in Summoner's Rift. It is located in the baron pit.

Slaying the **Rift Herald** drops the Eye of the Herald at the entrance of the pit for 20 seconds, which can be picked up by one member of the slayer's team, and is used for summoning the **Rift Herald Mercenary**.

The **Rift Herald** only spawns once per game, and despawns permanently at **19:45**, or **19:55** if in combat. Baron Nashor spawns in her place at **20:00**.

## Features

### General

- The level of the **Rift Herald** is the average of all champions' levels when she spawns, with a minimum of level 9.

- When the **Rift Herald** loses all patience, she will *hard reset*, immediately running back to her camp.

### Passives

- **Colossal Strength:** The **Rift Herald's** basic attacks deal 20% of target's **current** health **bonus** physical damage.

- **Cull the Weak:** The **Rift Herald** deals 50% **bonus** damage to non-champions.

- **Monstrous Toughness:** Effects that deal damage based on the **Rift Herald's** health and are not usually capped, are capped at 50 damage.

- ** Warped Armor:** The **Rift Herald** is immune to all crowd control, except stasis. Additionally, the **Rift Herald's** stats cannot be modified by any means.

- ** The Eye of Baron:** The **Rift Herald** has a vulnerable eye on her back. Champion attacks on-hit against it deal 12% of **Rift Herald's** **maximum** health **bonus** true damage. When the eye is hit, it closes for 6 seconds. The eye cannot open more than once every 3 seconds or if the **Rift Herald** is below 15% **maximum** health.

### Abilities

- **Charge:** At the start of the fight, the **Rift Herald** winds up for 2.5 seconds to leap forward, dealing 200% AD physical damage to enemies within her path and knocking them aside, though not through terrain.

  - While in the pit, she charges at the current location of the enemy who first damaged her.

- **Swipe:** At 65.75% and 32.75% of her **maximum** health, she winds up for 1.5 seconds to strike in a cone in front of her, dealing 125% AD physical damage to enemies hit.

The Eye of the Herald is a trinket that can be used to summon the **Rift Herald Mercenary** as an allied unit, which will relentlessly march down a lane to target enemy structures and push her way to the enemy Nexus. The summoned **Mercenary** has different statistics, effects and abilities than the pit **Rift Herald**.

### General

- She heads to the closest lane and starts pushing it.

- She aggroes the closest minions and *targetable* structures.

- Her level is the average of the levels of the champions of both teams the moment the Eye is cast.

- She grants kill credit to the champion that summoned her as well as to the slayer's allied champions who participated in her takedown as the pit **Rift Herald**.

  - Any units she kills and Turret Plating she destroys is accredited to these champions, granting them the kill gold.

### Passives

- Her basic attacks deal 1.75% of her **current** health **bonus** physical damage and knock back all enemy minions in front of her.

- ** The Eye of Baron:** Upon initiating combat and every 3 seconds thereafter during combat, the eye on the **Mercenary's** back will open. Champion basic attacks against the eye deal 40% of **Rift Herald's** **maximum** health **bonus** true damage.

### Abilities

- **Leap Attack:** When the **Mercenary** approaches a targetable structure, she winds up for 2.5 seconds to leap to her attack range of the structure. Upon arrival, she headbutts the structure, dealing 3000 true damage. For charges beyond the first, the charge damage is reduced to 66.67% / 44.44% / 29.63% / 19.75% / 13.17% / ...% (based on previous charges) of its original value. If the structure is successfully damaged, she loses 66% of her **current** health. Enemy champions near the impact of her attack are dealt 200% AD physical damage and knocked back.

  - Her dash toward the structure will be stopped prematurely if she collides with terrain (including player-generated terrain).

  - She is displacement immune during the wind up and the leap.

  - When the leap begins, enemies are alerted via an 'Assist Me' system ping.

- **Enrage:** At 65.75% and 32.75% of her **maximum** health, she winds up for 1.5 seconds to strike in a cone in front of her, dealing 125% AD physical damage to enemies hit.

- **Duchess Smash:** She disables the Reinforced Armor of nearby enemy turrets.

- ** Rift Herald Rodeo:** The **Mercenary** allows for one allied champion to initiate a charging attack. They can target her (default: Right Mouse Button MB2 / right-click) while within 600 range to wind up over 0.75 seconds, blinking to her current location and attaching herself to the user afterwards. Once attached, the player may choose the starting direction of their charge within 0.75 seconds via a Select or Move command (Left Mouse Button or Right Mouse Button, respectively). The champion then begins charging in the chosen direction while the **Mercenary** is attached to them for up to 12.5 seconds, automatically navigating their movement along the way. The player can steer the charge in the direction of their cursor with a turn rate that is increased over the first 3 seconds and decays every 2 seconds afterwards. Enemy champions hit by the **Mercenary** are dealt 250 area true damage, and knocked up and stunned for 1 second. The charge will stop if the **Mercenary** herself collides with an enemy structure or terrain, or the charge reaches the end of its duration, causing her to lose 66% of her **current** health, reduced to 13.2% if she hit terrain. When the charge stops, the champion will detach the **Mercenary** from themselves while simultaneously dashing 200 units away from behind her, increased to 700 units if the collision was against a targetable structure. The dash distance increases by  75% of the champion's **total** attack range.

  - Once the charge has ended, the **Mercenary** will resume her default, uncharged behavior.

  - The charge cannot be interrupted by crowd control.

  - Champions cannot initiate the charge while immobilized, grounded, or unable to cast abilities otherwise. They also cannot initiate it while in a zombie state.

  - During both the wind-up and the charge, the champion is rendered untargetable, invulnerable, displacement immune, and unable to act. They lose these effects 0.1 seconds after the charge ends.

  - If the charge collides with a targetable structure, the champion deals the same damage as if the **Mercenary** charged on her own, but additionally gains a shield for 4 seconds that decays linearly to 50% of its value over the duration and absorbs 550 damage. The **Mercenary** also spawns 5 (+ 1 – 6 (based on your team's Touch of the Void stacks)) allied Voidmites which will attack the nearest enemy structure.

    - Note that this damage is attributed to the champion, so it may be increased by any damage amplifiers they have (such as Haunting Guise's Madness).

  - The **Mercenary** becomes unable to act while the champion is winding up to initiate the charge.

  - If the player champion does not choose a starting direction for their charge, they will automatically charge in the **Mercenary's** facing direction.

  - For the first 3 seconds of the charge, the turn speed of the charge is significantly increased. The turn speed then decays every 2 seconds thereafter.

  - The charge's speed is determined by the champion's  movement speed.

    - The champion's movement speed is set to a static 150, which ramps up exponentially to 600 over the first 5 seconds. The value is increased by up to 75% while the charge is heading in the direction of an enemy structure, based on proximity to it, up to 1050 movement speed.

  - This effect cannot be used again until the **Mercenary** has assisted in the destruction of an enemy turret. It also cannot be initiated if the **Mercenary** is in combat with a structure.

    - An icon of the buff for this effect is shown next to her health bar to indicate that it is available.

    - If the player crashes the **Mercenary** onto terrain near a targetable structure within her *Leap Attack's* range, she will automatically attempt to charge that structure as compensation.

    - Attempting to activate the effect while it is disabled will report to the player that it is on cooldown.

  - If the **Mercenary** dies during this effect, the charge ends immediately, triggering the detachment without the dash and causing the champion to reappear at her death location.

## Notes

- The **Rift Herald Mercenary** loses her immunity to disables in summoned form.

  - However, her wind up on structures cannot be interrupted.

  - She is also not rendered immune to any crowd control during *Rift Herald Rodeo*. However, she technically cannot be displaced as she is being attached to the charging champion, overriding any forced movements imposed upon her with the movement of the champion she is attached to.

- The damage she inflicts to herself is internal raw damage.

- Turrets affected by Bard's Tempered Fate causes the **Rift Herald's** charge to have no effect (she won't damage the tower nor inflict damage to herself).

- She can be healed by Fiora's Victory Zone.

- She will not wind up her charge against a nearby turret that is untargetable.

- She can be teleported by Ryze's Realm Warp.

  - She can be teleported even while winding up on a structure or during her leap. She will still start her charge even after blinking during the windup (unless her windup completes during the 0.75 seconds where she is unable to act, in which case she will not start her charge).

- If she is in range of two targetable structures and her leap target is destroyed before her dash has started, she will automatically change her leap target to the next available structure instead. After casting one basic attack, she will then leap a second time toward the same target.

- **Rift Herald Rodeo's** charge collision damage to champions deals area damage, and thus will trigger spell effects.

## Strategy

- The **Mercenary** has a hefty amount of health and damage and several skills focused on taking down towers. The leap deals the same amount of damage regardless of current health, enabling the **Rift Herald** to rapidly destroy multiple turrets if left unchallenged.

- Since the **Mercenary** prioritizes minions and structures, it is not possible to distract it from smashing towers. The only way to stop the pushing is to take it down.

- The Mercenary can be dropped in a lane opposite to the team's currently targeted epic monster objective in order to distract enemies and force them to deal with its pushing power first.

- As Bel'Veth in True Form, the Mercenary combined with her *Void Remora* from converted minions create unmatched pushing power, so players should optimally aim to have True Form available and be prepared to push the lane when the Mercenary is summoned.

## Trivia

- The **Rift Herald** was once a Rift Scuttler that has since mutated due to the Void energy emanating from Baron Nashor's pit.

- The pit **Rift Herald** will dance if a champion dances nearby and she is idling. In her summoned form, she will dance if still alive when the game ends.

- In patch V7.9, it was stated that **Rift Heralds** were exclusively female.

- From patch V5.22 to patch V7.9, the **Rift Herald** had a wildly different appearance. It can be seen in the 'Skins' tab.

  - While she looked like this, her animations were recycled from the Blue Sentinel's.

    - Because of this, she shared its dance, which references Open the Door by Im Chang-jung.

      - She also shared her dance with Daisy, another unit whose animations were borrowed from the Blue Sentinel's.

Cc-Immune

**Crowd control immunity** prevents **any** type of crowd control from being *applied* to the unit. CC which has already been applied will still have an effect on the unit. Epic monsters (excluding a summoned **Rift Herald**) and turrets are always immune to all crowd control, with the exception of stasis.

Additional effects over the *crowd control* will not be prevented (e.g. Malzahar's Nether Grasp will not suppress but the tether still applies).

Stasis

A unit that is in *stasis* is unable to control its movement, declare attacks, cast abilities, activate items or use **any** summoner spells for the duration, while also being invulnerable and untargetable. ***Stasis* is an immobilizing effect**.

On-hit effect

**On-hit effects** refers to a category of effects that are applied on some damage instances, through the *TriggerOnHitEvents* property. Champion basic damage through their basic attack applies **on-hit effects** by default, but many other spells are made to do the same. The damage of most **on-hit effects** itself is in turn usually tagged proc, which is non-reactive (= does not trigger further damage-tag-specific effects).

Some important clarifications:

- A critical strike is not an on-hit effect. Their application is handled completely separately.

- Health restoration from  life steal is not an **on-hit effect**. However, damage can be made to apply life steal through the ApplyLifesteal property, and this is also employed on some **on-hit effects**.

**On-hit effects** contrast with ability effects, which apply on instances of ability damage.

Dash

A **dash** is a quick movement action by a unit in which they move to a specific direction or location while traversing the intervening distance, ignoring the boundaries of terrain.

The game engine does not take into account the height of the unit; dashes that depict air travel are considered to be on the ground for targeting purposes such as skill-shots.

- Dashes should not be confused with displacement effects, which are usually sourced from enemy units (and are classified as crowd control debuffs).

- Dashes are interrupted by stasis (from an enemy), knockdown, and blinks.

- Dashes are overridden by all move blocks, which includes displacements and other dashes, as well as a few other cases.

- Not all dashes can traverse terrain (e.g. Justice Punch, Searing Charge, Tumble).

- Some dashes gain increased distance when directed through terrain (e.g. Umbral Dash, Perfect Execution, Phantom Undertow).

Blink

A **blink** refers to a form of movement whereby a unit displaces from point A to B without traversing the space between.

Attached

**Attached** refers to the state of a unit whose movement is bound entirely to another object, such as a unit or missile. They will be at the current location of the unit they are attached to at all times, or at a specific offset to that location.

A unit can either attach themselves to another unit or attach another unit to themselves. In any case, the unit that has units attached to them can move those units by any means of movement, whether it be by pathing (movement commands), displacement ( dashes and forced displacement), or blinks. The unit that is being attached to another unit however cannot move from their current location by themselves by any means, including the use of the aforementioned movement methods.

Channel

A **channeled ability** is an ability that can be prevented from reaching its conclusion, by the user themself and / or because of other effects.

A **charged** ability has a channel that is toggled by two activations - the first begins the charging and the second will fire the ability. While using Quick cast, the player must keep the hotkey held down to charge the ability and release to fire. Unlike normal channels, the caster is still able to move freely and cast movement abilities, unless otherwise stated.

A **movement channel** is a type of channel which also involves the caster moving from their position, usually in the form of traversal such as dashes and blinks. This means the crowd control effects ground and root, which both inhibit the activation of movement spells, can interrupt the channel.

The following crowd control effects will interrupt all **channels** (except uninterruptible ones):

- Airborne

- Forced action ( Charm, Flee, Taunt)

- Silence ( Disrupt, Polymorph)

- Sleep

- Stasis

- Stun ( Suspension)

- Suppression

Area damage

**Area damage** (internally, AOE) is a secondary damage tag. Most champion, item and rune abilities that can hit targets within a zone are usually tagged **Area**.

This tag is generally restrictive and has the following interactions:

- Omnivamp heals with 33.3% effectiveness on instances of **area damage** against non-champions.

- Jax's Counter Strike grants 25% damage reduction against incoming **area damage**.

- Various champion summoned units such as Elise's Spiderlings, Naafiri's Packmates, and Yorick's Mist Walkers take reduced damage from **area damage** effects.

Effects triggered due to damage type ( physical, magic, true) will always occur regardless of classification (e.g. Black Cleaver's Carve stacking on instances of physical damage and Morellonomicon's Grievous Wounds application on instances of magic damage).

Untargetable

Raw damage

The term **raw damage** applies to a rare form of default damage that does not respect death protection and immunity effects on the target at all, such as invulnerability, minimum health threshold and resurrection. Spell shield, if applicable, is the only way to potentially avoid **raw damage** by negating the origin spell's effects entirely (which would include avoiding their damage).

**Raw damage** also does not interact with any effect. In the context of champions, the Nexus Obelisk against them and Zac's Cell Division's redirected damage are the only **raw damage** effects. Various other systems utilize it however, such as **Rift Herald's** self-damage.
