# Vamp

> ***Vamp**, especially **Physical vamp**, is not to be confused with Life steal.*

**Vamp** is a category of stat that regroups omnivamp, and drain, as well as physical vamp, and spell vamp.

## Omnivamp

** Omnivamp** is a stat which grants healing equal to a percentage of the post-mitigation physical damage, magic damage, and true damage dealt. It stacks additively and does not benefit from  heal and shield power.

Omnivamp is reduced to 33% effectiveness against minions and monsters with area, pet, or persistent damage.

Omnivamp does not apply to damage dealt by Ignite and Smite.

*Gold Value*

- **Omnivamp** has a gold value of 46 per percent point.

## Notes

- Grievous Wounds will reduce the healing received from all sources of vamp.

- Spirit Visage's Boundless Vitality specifically can increase healing received from all sources of vamp, as it is not true  heal and shield power.

  - The bonus will apply to vamp multiplicatively; for example, 20% vamp will be modified to 25% vamp (20% × 1.25 = 25%).

- Reactive damage (damage type exclusively used by Bramble Vest and Thornmail) does not benefit from any vamp effects.

- Proc damage (i.e., on-hit effects), default damage and basic damage will not benefit from spell vamp, since they do not apply spell effects.

- Although some abilities may be unit-targeted, that does not mean they never deal area damage; Conflagration deals area of effect damage to all enemies, despite targeting only one unit.

  - **New Effect:** Now has a healing penalty for damage dealt to minions and monsters, at 20% effectiveness.

  - **Undocumented / Estimated:** Physical vamp is no longer a visible stat on the HUD.

  - With the removal of Sanguine Blade, there are no sources of physical vamp left in the game.

**V10.23**

- Life steal

  - **New Effect:** Certain on-hit effects from items now benefit from life steal.

    - The following items benefit from life steal: Ardent Censer, Black Cleaver, Blade of the Ruined King, Divine Sunderer, Essence Reaver, Guinsoo's Rageblade, Lich Bane, Muramana, Nashor's Tooth, Rageknife, Recurve Bow, Sheen, Titanic Hydra, Trinity Force, Wit's End, Zeke's Convergence.

  - Physical vamp applies to all physical damage dealt.

  - Omnivamp applies to all damage dealt, including magic damage, physical damage, and true damage.

Stacks additively

An effect that **stacks additively** can be calculated by adding the effects together.

Most champion statistics that stack this way are expressed through flat numbers, but the stats can also be percentages. The following stats **stack additively**:

- Ability haste

- Ability power

- Armor

- Attack damage

 League of Legends content

Area damage

**Area damage** (internally, AOE) is a secondary damage tag. Most champion, item and rune abilities that can hit targets within a zone are usually tagged **Area**.

This tag is generally restrictive and has the following interactions:

- Omnivamp heals with 33.3% effectiveness on instances of **area damage** against non-champions.

- Jax's Counter Strike grants 25% damage reduction against incoming **area damage**.

- Various champion summoned units such as Elise's Spiderlings, Naafiri's Packmates, and Yorick's Mist Walkers take reduced damage from **area damage** effects.

Effects triggered due to damage type ( physical, magic, true) will always occur regardless of classification (e.g. Black Cleaver's Carve stacking on instances of physical damage and Morellonomicon's Grievous Wounds application on instances of magic damage).

 League of Legends content

Pet damage

**Pet damage** is a secondary damage tag. Most basic attacks of champion summoned units are tagged **Pet**.

**Pet damage** is used primarily for damage attribution to their summoners. It is excluded from applying Elixir of Sorcery's damage on turrets via this attribution.

Effects triggered by damage subtypes ( physical, magic, true) will always occur regardless of damage type (e.g. Black Cleaver's Carve stacking on instances of physical damage and Morellonomicon's Grievous Wounds application on instances of magic damage).

 League of Legends content

Persistent damage

**Persistent damage** (internally, Periodic) is a secondary damage tag. Most damage-over-time (DoT) and repeatedly firing effects are usually tagged **Persistent**.

This tag is generally restrictive. Various persistent damage effects that interact with other damage types are time-gated so that they do not continuously trigger on or from the target. Additionally, **persistent damage** cannot interrupt sleep.

Effects triggered due to damage type ( physical, magic, true) will always occur regardless of classification (e.g. Black Cleaver's Carve stacking on instances of physical damage and Morellonomicon's Grievous Wounds application on instances of magic damage).

Reactive damage

*Reactive damage* is a damage type that is currently exclusive to Bramble Vest and its upgrade Thornmail.

*Reactive damage* does not interact with most other effects, such as Luden's Echo or Arcane Comet. The main exception is:

- Omnivamp heals for a percentage of damage dealt.

Effects triggered by damage subtypes ( physical, magic, true) will always occur regardless of damage type (e.g. Black Cleaver's Carve stacking on instances of physical damage and Morellonomicon's Grievous Wounds application on instances of magic damage).

 League of Legends content

Proc damage

**Proc damage** is a primary damage tag. Effects that should not interact with anything else are usually tagged **Proc**.

Generic interactions can still trigger from **proc damage**, such as minion aggression and healing from damage dealt via  omnivamp or  physical vamp (with the appropriate properties).

Effects triggered due to damage type ( physical, magic, true) will always occur regardless of classification (e.g. Black Cleaver's Carve stacking on instances of physical damage and Morellonomicon's Grievous Wounds application on instances of magic damage).

 League of Legends content

On-hit effect

**On-hit effects** refers to a category of effects that are applied on some damage instances, through the *TriggerOnHitEvents* property. Champion basic damage through their basic attack applies **on-hit effects** by default, but many other spells are made to do the same. The damage of most **on-hit effects** itself is in turn usually tagged proc, which is non-reactive (= does not trigger further damage-tag-specific effects).

Some important clarifications:

- A critical strike is not an on-hit effect. Their application is handled completely separately.

- Health restoration from  life steal is not an **on-hit effect**. However, damage can be made to apply life steal through the ApplyLifesteal property, and this is also employed on some **on-hit effects**.

**On-hit effects** contrast with ability effects, which apply on instances of ability damage.
