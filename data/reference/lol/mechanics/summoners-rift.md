**Summoner's Rift** is the first and most popular gameplay map for League of Legends. It is used in several game modes which includes permanent Classic queues, and it is the only map used to determine the winners of tournament matches across all professional  Esports leagues.

The map was given a graphical and technical update on patch 1.0.0.140 and remade from scratch before Season 2015, becoming available for open beta testing on November 12th, 2014 (during patch 4.19). The look of Summoner's Rift changes with the current thematic season, and additional textures, models and decals may be inserted during tier 1 Esports tournaments, real-life seasons, and sometimes other events, as part of praise/celebration.

The Summoner's Rift announcer can be viewed on the Announcers page.

## Environment

Summoner's Rift Map (Top Down View)

Summoner's Rift is a timeless and mythical battleground set within a square-shaped field and serves purely as an idealized location where two teams of legendary *champions* are meant to clash. The ground on the inner portion of the field consists mostly of grass and dirt, and a forest spans the majority of the area. At two opposite corners of the map, stone platforms are laid down which form two sizeable zones; they are carved with intricate patterns. A wide, slightly curved river flows along a line between the other two corners. Irregular rocky and muddy walls separate and enclose the many points of interest found on the field. The flora consists of mostly trees and brushes. Diverse critters abound near, on or within the rocky walls and in the river and forest, while manmade artifacts and symbols from across Runeterra can be seen scattered throughout the field, some changing as the seasons do.

The blue (southwest) side represents the concept of **Order**, whereas the red (northeast) side represents the concept of **Chaos**. These denominations are also officially used for all *League of Legends* maps where there are two teams starting on opposite sides of the map. Due to its real-life history—that being the history of the game itself—some pieces of old content from the legacy lore and legacy events persist on Summoner's Rift.

## Gameplay

### General

Fight Against Baron Nashor.

Summoner's Rift features three open lanes where waves of minions march down, starting at each team's *base* in two opposite corners of the map, where their side's spawn point, shop and important structures lie. Each side is guarded by a number of destructible structures that provide an innate area of control to each team. Going from the middle of a lane toward either base, there is one Outer turret, one Inner turret, one Inhibitor turret and an Inhibitor; the lane ends at the team's Nexus deep within the base, which is itself guarded by just two Nexus turrets. Each team's base is fenced off from the rest of the map by two thin and long walls set in an arc arrangement, though base gates exist at their midpoint which allow passage to that side's players only.

A *river* crosses between the other two corners of the map opposite to the teams' bases, though not through the lanes.

The area outside of the bases, lanes, and river is permeated by wall-dense *jungle*. The jungle is home to all neutral monster camps, which have varying degrees of toughness.

Other small destructible objects can exist throughout the map which are called *plants*. Most of those plants are inside the jungle. Each grants a unique gameplay effect.

### Elemental Rift

> *Main article: Dragon pit: Elemental Rift*

The spawning of the third elemental drake signals a shift on the map called *Elemental Rift*. The features are based on the prevailing drake element and can include terrain changes, additional terrain or gameplay features and plant changes. In total, there are six different elemental drakes and thus map variations: Chemtech Rift, Cloud Rift, Hextech Rift, Infernal Rift, Mountain Rift, and Ocean Rift.

### Corrupted Rift

> *Main article: Baron pit: Corrupted Rift*

The first spawn of Baron Nashor signals a map shift as well, causing its camp pit's terrain as well as the Blue Sentinel and Red Brambleback monsters to be permanently altered by the Void.

The current shopkeepers of Summoner's Rift are unnamed and so generically described as a **Female Yordle** (blue side) and a **Male Otter** (red side). They are mute; the former however is voiced on the Koeshin's Crossing map. The yordle is accompanied by a large draft animal resembling a boar, her counter being assembled on its tail. The male otter stands on the deck of a punctured hot air balloon. Various items that players can actually buy from the shop can be seen around both shop stalls.

## Versions

### Winter Version

Snowdown Showdown Summoner's Rift

The Winter Map is a variant of the regular Summoner's Rift designed for the 2009 Snowdown Showdown event, but stayed for approximately a year. It was replaced by the Autumn Map in November 2010. It was also available during the 2010 Snowdown Showdown, replacing the Autumn version temporarily and made a return for the 2011 Snowdown Showdown in December 2011. Riot released an updated version of the map for the duration of the 2016 Snowdown Showdown event. In late 2025, during Trials of Twilight Act II, the Winter Map was overhauled; it supports all Elemental Rift transformations and features various new Easter eggs. The Winter Map has the following differences with the regular Summoner's Rift:

- A mostly white background, because of the snow.

- Snow falling and covering the terrain.

- The River is frozen.

- Presents and Poros can be seen throughout the map.

- Baron Nashor wears a Santa hat, earmuffs and mittens.

- The Rift Herald wears green ribbons, a red nose, fake antlers and sleigh bells.

- Atakhan wears a Santa themed clothing and has horns.

- The Voidgrubs and Voidmites are dressed as an elf and present respectively

### Autumn Version

Harrowing Summoner's Rift

The Autumn Version of Summoner's Rift was a special variant designed for the 2010 Harrowing seasonal Event. It replaced the Winter Version as the secondary version available and renamed Autumn Version after a poll in November 2010. It was temporarily replaced again with the Winter Version for the 2010 Snowdown Showdown. The Autumn Version uses music from the Twisted Treeline.

- There are coffins at bottom right area of the map. These signify the "death" (cancellation) of several champions.

### Blood Moon Version

The Blood Moon Version of Summoner's Rift was a special variant designed for the Hunt of the Blood Moon game mode, released in the 2017  Blood Moon event (V7.3).

### Arcade Version

The Arcade Version of Summoner's Rift was a special variant designed for the August 2017  Arcade event (V7.16).

## Trivia

- Art concepts for Summoner Rift are usually done before Riot make 3D in-game models. Sometimes they will make a concept, but it does not work in the game. Examples: the design makes the map hard to see and navigate; it does not fit the environment; it does not look tanky or weak enough. The creatures have to look the appropriate power level in relation to each other. An environment piece has to be visually appealing, but not distracting compared to champions' and other gameplay animations and effects.

- The distance from the blue team's **Nexus Obelisk** to the red team's is about 20500 units of distance.

- A Raptor, Forest chameleon, and Antlermice can be seen in Kog'Maw's classic splash art, which are creatures that reside in Summoner's Rift.

- Historically, the map represents the war between two world-ruling factions, the Protectorate (Order) and the Magelords (Chaos). These were also showcased in Aatrox's first retcon.

  - Later on, the Institute of War lore was transformed into an alternate universe of the same name ( Institute of War) where members of the Protectorate would be represented by Wardens and Magelords by Marauders.

  - The owl symbol of the Magelords looks similar to the Porowl, a species of owls that live in Summoner's Rift.

### Summoner's Rift Update

Retextured (left) and Original (right) Summoner's Rift

May 23, 2012 brought graphical and technical updates to Summoner's Rift. The updates brought players better aesthetics as well as improved overall graphical performance for players on lower settings. Some of the enhancements included:

- Better game performance at low and mid spec.

- Updated textures across Summoner's Rift.

- Improved animations on many of the monsters, including the Ancient Golem, the Lizard Elder and, of course, Baron Nashor.

- A new, animated shopkeeper to service all of your champion's item purchases.

### Summoner's Rift Evolves

Champions fighting the Blue Sentinel and Sentries

Champions fighting the Blue Sentinel and Sentries

Champions fighting the Red Brambleback

Champions fighting the Red Brambleback

On November 12, 2014 a new update to Summoner's Rift went live for use on the Team Builder queue and all bot modes. One week later, on November 19, the updated ("Evolved") Summoner's Rift was released for all queues, replacing the old one entirely. This time the update has been more heavier than the previous one, and the map was remade from scratch, changing the overall art style. From the minions, to the monsters, the structures, the decorations, the vegetation and even the music it was all completely remade. The new Summoner's Rift is in all respects a new map, made preserving the spirit and gameplay of the older Summoner's Rift. The new elements are:

**General**

- A new handcrafted artistic style to Summoner's Rift.

- Gameplay on the map is more readable than in the previous map. Making action the top priority on the screen rather than the environment.

- Cleaned up a lot of the terrain (so that the widths of the visuals match the width of the mesh).

  - This is particularly noticeable in Red-side jungle.

- *Purple Team* changed to *Red Team*.

- Red base features an owl and mage motif with curved linear shapes. Blue base is inspired by a stag and paladin theme with blockier shapes. Both of the owl and stag sigils meet in middle lane.

- Fog of war upgrade - fog is darker and the fade between fog and non-fog is now more abrupt/crisp (it's not a gradual fade).

- General bug fixes.

- Camera angle adjusted to be more similar to Howling Abyss (higher angled, less perspective).

  - This should make the difference between playing up and down less significant.

- Large and Epic Jungle monsters now show their current health value above their health bar.

- Bottom and top lane brushes are now centered between their respective towers (previously off-centered by ~450 units).

- Ambient critters have been added in the map.

- New minion and monster icons.

- New Kill indicators on screen (including a hexakill indicator).

- Updated look to minions, cannon minions and super minions.

  - Red minions wield axes while blue minions war hammers.

- Two new shopkeepers for both of the Bases.

- Each jungle quadrant has a unique theme. Blue buff jungle has a blue hue, while the Red jungle has a red hue. West jungle is more destroyed by the influence of Baron Nashor while the east one retains its more natural look.

- New Summoner's Rift music.

**Jungle**

- The Ancient Golem and its Lizard henchmen were replaced with the Blue Sentinel golem and two monolithic Sentries.

  - The Sentries were removed in the Preseason 2017 jungle update.

- The Lizard Elder camp is now inhabited by the Red Brambleback, a fiery ent-like creature, and two Cinderlings.

  - The Cinderlings were removed in the Preseason 2017 jungle update.

- The Golem and Big Golem have been replaced by the Krug and Ancient Krug, respectively.

- The Giant Wolf and the Lesser Wolves have been respectively replaced with the Greater Murk Wolf and the Murk Wolves.

- The Wraiths have been replaced with bird-like Raptors led by a Crimson Raptor.

- The Wight has been replaced with the Gromp, a walrus-frog hybrid.

- Dragon and Baron Nashor have received Visual updates and kit reworks.

- A new passive monster has been added in the north and south parts of the river, Rift Scuttler.

- Plants were added to the jungle in Season 2017: Blast Cones airborne knocks units back, Scryer's Bloom provides vision, and Honeyfruit restores health and mana.

Classic

**Classic Mode** refers to the game where the primary goal is to destroy the enemy Nexus.

Defending each Nexus are several turrets and inhibitors; each structure blocks the advancement of enemy *minion waves* and *champions* to the Nexus, and must be destroyed in sequence for each lane. Destroying the last structure alive in at least one lane grants access to the Nexus. *Turrets right before the Nexus do **not** correspond to any specific lane*.

- A **turret** (or **tower**) is a heavy fortification that attacks enemies on sight.

- An **inhibitor** does not attack, but when a lane's inhibitor is destroyed, the opposite Nexus will spawn ***super minions*** within the same lane, allowing for even faster advancement.

Turret

A **turret**, also called a **tower**, is a fortification that defends a part of territory. It provides vision of the area and autonomously fires homing bolts at nearby enemies, establishing a defensive ground for allies.

Turrets are most notable in Classic, defending against waves of enemy Minions from their ultimate goal of destroying the nexus. Champions work to destroy enemy turrets to push their assault into the enemies' territory. Turrets visually fall apart as they take damage.

Airborne

A unit that is knocked *airborne* undergoes a *forced displacement* in a specified direction, to a specific point, or for a set duration at its current location, during which it is also unable to control its movement, declare attacks, cast abilities, activate items or use the summoner spells Flash, Teleport, Hexflash, and Mark / Dash. ***Airborne* is an immobilizing effect**.

| Interrupts: | Interrupts attack windups (excluding uncancellable windups), and all channeled and charged abilities. The accompanying *displacement* overrides all other previous *displacements*, including dashes and other *airborne* effects. |
| --- | --- |
| Reduction: | The **duration** of *airborne* and its *forced movement* execution time is unaffected by Tenacity. |
| Resist: | Resisted by crowd control immunity, displacement immunity, and Dr. Mundo's Goes Where He Pleases. |
| Removal: | The forced movement that is applied alongside *airborne* effects cannot be removed by cleanses, which innately disables attacking and moving and can be overridden by dashing, blinking or lunging. Cleansing effects, with the exceptions of Milio's Breath of Life, Cleanse, Mikael's Blessing, and Quicksilver, remove the *airborne* status effect. |

Crowd control applied by one's self or an ally cannot be countered in any way, neither *reduced* in duration, *resisted*, nor *removed* unless special cased to do so.

- The crowd control effect may still be cleansed if it is applied as a debuff separate from the originated ability.
