# Kill

> *For information on **champion gold bounties**, see this article.*

A **kill** is the event of inflicting the final blow on an enemy unit, reducing their health to zero. This is usually accompanied by the slain unit dispensing an  experience and gold **bounty** to the killer, their assistors, and/or nearby allies, depending on the unit. Champion kills increase a team's and a player's Kill score, while minion, monster and summon kills increase a player's Creep score. Turret and epic monster kills are tracked in the scoreboard for each team.

When a champion is slain, they enter the death state, where they will *respawn* at their fountain after a certain amount of time. If the champion is protected by certain effects (e.g. resurrection), reducing their health to zero does not count as a kill. The first champion kill to happen by a champion in the game is called **First Blood**.

Kills and assists together are called *takedowns*, which may be scored to trigger various beneficial effects.

## Champion kill credit

Champions uniquely do not need to score the final blow on enemy champions in order to take credit for killing them.

A champion is credited a kill on an enemy champion so long as they have affected them with any of the following valid effects within **15** seconds in Summoner's Rift or **20** seconds in the Howling Abyss – even if a non-champion has actually reduced the enemy's health to zero in the end:

- Any damage (attacks, abilities, summoner spells, item effects or debuffs). Zero-damage spells such as marking the enemy with Zilean's Time Bomb all count as well.

- Any form of crowd control, excluding blind and cripple (none of which need to be accompanied by damage).

  - The slow of Mordekaiser's Realm of Death does not count as crowd control, but entering the Death Realm does.^(bug)

- The summoner spell Exhaust (which does not deal damage).

- A damaging turret basic attack after the turret has been affected by an allied Bard's Tempered Fate.

- Enclosing the enemy within Veigar's Event Horizon from the start of the cast time (which does not deal damage).

The timer refreshes with each new application of a valid effect, and kill credit is granted to the last player to have affected the enemy within that timer before the enemy's death. Any other players that have affected the slain enemy with a valid kill-crediting effect, or with a few other indirect effects are credited an **assist** instead. Assists on champion kills are subject to the same timer from the moment of the enemy's death: if a player has contributed longer ago than the timer, they will not be credited an assist, regardless of how long the combat between the killer and the slain enemy lasted.

### Execution

If a minion, monster or turret inflicts the final blow on a champion who has not been affected by an enemy champion within the timer, the slain enemy is **executed** instead.

An executed champion's gold bounty is not dispensed nor affected, and their kill streak is not interrupted.

## Rewards

### Experience and gold bounties

The primary rewards for killing a unit are  experience and gold bounties. These are dispensed in different ways by different units and in different modes. For example:

- In Classic Summoner's Rift, being within a certain range of a minion death will split its experience bounty among nearby allies, but will only grant a share of its experience and its full gold bounty to the player that last-hits it regardless of range and death status. In ARAM, nearby minion deaths always generate some gold.

- Turrets grant no experience, but split a gold bounty to the killer and any recent assistors or nearby allies who are alive, and also grant a smaller amount of gold to the team globally. The Rift Herald Mercenary can be summoned to gain the total amount of gold regardless of range instead.

- Most jungle monsters grant both their experience and gold bounty to the killer, but epic monsters may be different.

- Ward kills grant no experience and only dispense gold to the killer.

- Jungle plant kills grant no experience nor gold.

- *And so on.*

In terms of killing champions, solo and assisted kills grant different amounts of experience and gold bounties. Both the experience and gold bounties for champions may be increased or decreased based on the advantage or disadvantage between the participant(s) and the slain champion. The experience bounty is dispensed and shared equally among the killer, assistors as well as nearby allies. The killer receives the slain champion's full gold bounty, up to a cap, whereas additional gold is generated if the kill was assisted and is split equally among all assistors.

For further reading on experience bounties from any source, see the Experience (champion) article. For champion and objective gold bounties, see Champion gold bounties and Objective bounties.

### Buffs

All epic monsters and certain large monsters grant powerful buffs to the killer and/or the ally team, which may be temporary or permanent. Certain map elements, such as Blood Roses, can also reward permanent buffs for their destruction.

If the slain champion was holding Crest of Cinders and/or Crest of Insight, they are transferred to the killer and additionally have their duration reset. The transferring will not take place if the buff was earned from a *Corrupted* monster, however.

## Multi-kill

A multi-kill is the act of killing several champions within 10 seconds of each other, with this window refreshing on scoring a kill. After scoring a Quadra Kill, the window for scoring a Pentakill increases to 30 seconds; if an enemy respawns before this time, the window ends prematurely.

Special announcements play for champions who score multi-kills: "<Player1> has slain <Player2> for a <Multi-kill>!"

- Double Kill (2)

- Triple Kill (3)

- Quadra Kill (4)

- Pentakill (5)

- Hexakill (6), only on the Hexakill mode

**Notes**

- The window for a pentakill also has a minimum of 10 seconds, just like any other kill in a multi-kill sequence. This is not currently applicable in most game modes however.

- A multi-kill of six or more champions outside of Hexakill mode is not announced. Even if a champion manages to kill seven champions within the time window, the seventh kill is not announced as a Double Kill. It would be accurately recorded in the "Largest Multi Kill" in the post-match stats, however. The kill sound effect of a Pentakill is played for every additional kill in the multi-kill after 5.

**History**

> Some footage from before the time Hexakill was introduced showcases the possibility of achieving a *Legendary Kill*, which can occur after killing another enemy after a Pentakill, on any gamemode other than **Hexakill**. However, this feature seems to have been removed from the game, and the time at which it occurred is uncertain.

## Ace

An **ace** is scored by killing the last living champion of the enemy team. Scoring an ace is far easier than scoring a Pentakill, since the only requisite is for the entire allied team to kill the enemy team, rather than one player to kill the entire enemy team. There is no direct bonus associated with an ace, but it is accompanied by a unique announcement that credits the player who scored it.

Both teams can be aced at the same time, leaving no champions alive on the map.

Resurrection

A unit that is protected by **Resurrection** will enter a revival state and dispel most debuffs upon taking fatal damage. After the state completes, the unit is revived. **Resurrection** effects do not count for kills or assists.

**Resurrection** sources have a hierarchy, and will trigger in the following order:

- Zilean's Chronoshift

- Zaahen's Cultivation of War

- Guardian Angel

- Anivia's Rebirth and Zac's Cell Division

**Resurrection** will always take precedence over zombie state effects. Entering **resurrection** does not cancel cast times. Renata Glasc's Bailout always takes priority over **resurrection**.

Blind

A unit that is *blinded* will *miss* their basic attacks for the duration, comparable to attacking a target that is able to dodge or block, but not mechanically similar. *Missed* basic attacks will fail to deal damage and apply on-hit effects, with the exception of some empowered basic attacks. *Blind* does not prevent the unit from declaring attacks, unlike **Disarm**.

| Removal: | Removed by Cleanses with the exception of Mikael's Blessing. |

Cripple

A unit that is *crippled* has their **total**  attack speed reduced by a percentage for the duration.

Sources of *cripple* stack multiplicatively with each other.

Negative *cripple* values will increase the unit's attack speed instead.

| Interrupts: | Does not interrupt any actions. |
| --- | --- |
| Reduction: | The **duration** is affected by Tenacity. |
| Resist: | Resisted by crowd control immunity and cripple immunity. |
| Removal: | Removed by Cleanses and specifically Master Yi's Highlander. |

Crowd control applied by one's self or an ally cannot be countered in any way, neither *reduced* in duration, *resisted*, nor *removed* unless special cased to do so.

- The crowd control effect may still be cleansed if it is applied as a debuff separate from the originated ability.

Slow

A unit that is *slowed* has reduced  movement speed for the duration. *Slows* based on a percentage value cannot reduce a target below  110 movement speed. Some *slows* will instead modify the target's movement speed to a static value, which may not be further reduced nor increased.

Only the slow with the highest value is taken into movement speed calculations. All additional slows are ignored until the higher-valued slow wears off. The strength of slows may amplify or decay over their duration. Slows of the same debuff cannot stack and will instead refresh on each application unless otherwise stated.

Negative *slow* values will increase the unit's movement speed instead.

| Interrupts: | Does not interrupt any actions. |
| --- | --- |
| Reduction: | The **duration** is affected by Tenacity.The **strength** is reduced by slow resist. |
| Resist: | Resisted by crowd control immunity and slow immunity. |
| Removal: | Removed by Cleanses, Garen's Decisive Strike, Master Yi's Highlander and Nunu's Biggest Snowball Ever!. |

Classic

**Classic Mode** refers to the game where the primary goal is to destroy the enemy Nexus.

Defending each Nexus are several turrets and inhibitors; each structure blocks the advancement of enemy *minion waves* and *champions* to the Nexus, and must be destroyed in sequence for each lane. Destroying the last structure alive in at least one lane grants access to the Nexus. *Turrets right before the Nexus do **not** correspond to any specific lane*.

- A **turret** (or **tower**) is a heavy fortification that attacks enemies on sight.

- An **inhibitor** does not attack, but when a lane's inhibitor is destroyed, the opposite Nexus will spawn ***super minions*** within the same lane, allowing for even faster advancement.

A unit that is **Invulnerable** reduces all damage taken to **0** and prevents effects that trigger from damage.

Minimum health threshold

A unit under the effect of a **minimum health threshold** cannot have their health reduced below it by common means such as damage. The following grant this effect:

- Kindred's Lamb's Respite

- Pantheon's Grand Starfall

- Tryndamere's Undying Rage

Executes will reduce the health of units with this state to **1** health. Other sources of raw damage will ignore this state.

Hexakill

Hexakill is essentially a 6v6 match. The name comes from the new multi-kill possible with six players, where the **Hexakill** will be announced when one player scores six kills in quick succession.

It was initially played on Summoner's Rift, but a new version of this game mode (enabled in V4.18) compacts the players into the Twisted Treeline. To compensate for the greater number of players on the map, minions and experience scale up more quickly, and the control of both altars provide buffs to minions as well.
